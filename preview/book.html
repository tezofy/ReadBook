Chapter One

The auction had ended at half past four and by six the Dalloway estate had emptied of everything portable except the woman in the gallery.

Elias March had not intended to buy a house. He had come to buy a painting — a small, vicious thing in oil depicting a woman with her throat cut and her expression of mild surprise, which he had wanted since he was nineteen and had never been able to explain why. The estate had come with the painting. The estate had come with the grounds, the debt, the three hundred rooms that needed roofs, and a woman who was standing very still in a doorway as though she had been placed there by an interior decorator.

“You are still here,” she said.

“So are you.”

She had not been told he was coming. That was the first thing he noticed, and the second was that she had not been frightened, which meant she had already decided what he was. Everyone else in the house had been frightened of him. The solicitors had been frightened. The house itself seemed faintly frightened, the way old houses do when they sense they are about to be sold.

She was the only one who looked at him as though he were a problem she had already solved.
